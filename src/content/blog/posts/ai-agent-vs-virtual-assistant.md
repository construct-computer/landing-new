Hiring a virtual assistant used to mean one decision: which agency or freelancer. Now it means a second one first - human VA, or a supervised AI agent that does the same job for a fraction of the monthly bill. This post puts real numbers next to each option, concedes where the human still wins, and shows where an AI employee's audit trail closes the gap a chat-based "AI VA" cannot.

## What a virtual assistant does vs what an AI employee does

A human virtual assistant handles inbox triage, calendar management, data entry, light research, travel booking, and customer or client communication. The value is judgment applied to a known playbook: a good VA learns your preferences, catches exceptions, and represents you in conversations that require tone and tact.

An **AI employee** takes on many of the same tasks but executes them directly inside your connected applications instead of describing the steps back to you. Construct is built around that distinction. Point it at a scheduled inbox triage job, for example, and it opens the connected inbox, sorts new messages against the rules you gave it, drafts replies to the ones that match a known pattern, sends them, and leaves the rest flagged for a human decision. Every one of those actions, and the reasoning behind flagging a message instead of answering it, shows up afterward in Activity history, not just a summary claiming the work got done. If you have not seen the term before, [what is an AI employee](/blog/what-is-an-ai-employee) covers the concept in full.

It is worth separating this from a plain chat assistant, too. A tool that only answers questions in a chat window is not doing a VA's job, no matter how good the answers are; see [chat assistants vs AI employees](/blog/chat-assistants-vs-ai-employees) if that is the comparison you actually meant to make. And if the "VA" work you are trying to replace is really a fixed sequence of triggers and actions rather than judgment calls, a deterministic automation platform may fit better than either option here, which is the case made in [Construct vs Zapier](/vs/zapier).

## Cost comparison: monthly VA spend vs a supervised AI agent

### What a human VA actually costs in 2026

Human VA pricing varies by where the person is based and how the engagement is structured. Hourly rates run $5 to $75 depending on location and skill level, and a part-time arrangement covering daily email and calendar management typically runs $1,000 to $3,000 a month, according to Tendem's breakdown of virtual assistant costs (source: [tendem.ai](https://tendem.ai/blog/virtual-assistant-vs-ai-agent-which-do-you-need)).

Full-time engagements show a wide domestic-versus-offshore split. Cherry Assistant puts an offshore managed agency at $640 to $2,400 a month full-time, against $4,700 to $8,300 a month as the realistic total cost of a US-based in-house assistant once salary, benefits, payroll taxes, and recruiting are included (source: [cherryassistant.com](https://www.cherryassistant.com/blog/how-much-does-a-virtual-assistant-cost)). Stealth Agents' research on Filipino VA agencies lands in a similar range: $700 to $2,500 a month all-in for a managed agency hire, versus $4,800 to $12,000 a month for a comparable US-based hire (source: [stealthagents.com](https://stealthagents.com/research/cost-of-hiring-a-virtual-assistant-in-the-philippines-2026)).

Two more numbers matter beyond the sticker price. A structured offshore executive VA onboarding typically reaches full productivity between day 14 and day 30, and a rushed one can take 60 to 90 days or end in early turnover (source: [outsourcedscale.com](https://www.outsourcedscale.com/blog/how-to-onboard-an-offshore-executive-virtual-assistant-so-they-perform-from-day-one/)). Once someone is up to speed, outsourced VA teams still see about 18% annual turnover, and each replacement costs an estimated $3,600 to $5,400 in lost productivity and rehiring (source: [365outsource.com](https://www.365outsource.com/virtual-assistance/virtual-assistant-retention-hiring-strategy/)).

### Where the gap comes from, and where it narrows

The structural cost difference shows up per interaction, not just per month. AI handles a routine interaction for under $0.50, against $4 to $8 for a human agent, roughly an 11x gap per task (source: [stealthagents.com](https://stealthagents.com/research/ai-virtual-assistant-vs-human-virtual-assistant-statistics-2026)). That gap is why recurring, well-defined work (inbox triage, status updates, research pulls, CRM upkeep) is where a supervised AI agent pays for itself fastest.

The gap narrows on anything that needs relationship context, negotiation, or judgment calls a playbook cannot fully specify, which is exactly the work covered in the next section. It also does not mean demand for human VAs is shrinking: the global virtual assistant market is valued at $8.11 billion in 2025 and is projected to reach $10.11 billion in 2026, a 24.7% CAGR for that year, then continue at a 24.1% CAGR through 2030, reaching $23.97 billion (source: [researchandmarkets.com](https://www.researchandmarkets.com/reports/5951793/virtual-assistant-market-report)). Both categories are growing at once, because they are increasingly suited to different halves of the same job description.

## Where a human VA still wins

Concede this plainly: for interactions that depend on tone, empathy, or reading a client's mood, a human still outperforms an AI agent today. Human-handled interactions score 88% satisfaction against 60% for AI-handled ones, a 28-point gap that has not closed in recent tracking, and 93.4% of US consumers say they prefer a human for customer service work (source: [stealthagents.com](https://stealthagents.com/research/ai-virtual-assistant-vs-human-virtual-assistant-statistics-2026)).

That preference tracks with how the executive assistant role is actually evolving, not disappearing. As Oana Manolache, founder and CEO of Sequel.io, put it: "it doesn't replace what an executive assistant does now as the role has evolved" (source: [fortune.com](https://fortune.com/2026/07/05/ai-displacement-executive-assistants-secretaries-skills-adaptation/)). High-stakes client calls, sensitive HR conversations, in-person coordination, and any relationship where the other party specifically wants to be talking to a person are still a human VA's territory, and no pricing table changes that.

## Where a supervised AI agent wins: speed, availability, and an audit trail

A supervised AI agent starts the same day you sign up, works around the clock, and does not carry onboarding ramp time, turnover risk, or replacement cost. Construct runs at that structural cost advantage on every routine task, and it does not lose institutional knowledge when a contract ends, because memory persists and can be inspected rather than walking out the door with the person who held it; see [AI agent memory](/ai-agent-memory) for how that works.

The wedge a chat-based "AI VA" cannot match is the audit trail. A chatbot that drafts a reply for you to copy and send leaves no record of what actually happened in your inbox, calendar, or CRM. Construct's Activity history logs the steps an agent took and, where available, why a tool was used, across email, calendar, files, terminal, browser, and more than 1,000 connected applications. That record is what makes delegating recurring work to an agent something you can actually supervise instead of something you have to take on faith.

This is also a realistic moment to size the category correctly rather than oversell it. McKinsey's 2025 State of AI survey found that even among the most advanced adopters, no more than 10% of organizations report scaling agentic AI within any single business function, while 23% are actively scaling in at least one function and 39% are still experimenting (source: [forbes.com](https://www.forbes.com/sites/josipamajic/2026/03/22/10-of-enterprise-functions-use-ai-agents-mckinsey-finds/)). Supervised, bounded use on specific recurring tasks is where the category actually is in 2026, not full autonomous replacement of a VA's entire job.

## Choosing between the two, or running both together

Match the tool to the task rather than the category. Recurring, cross-app work with a clear procedure is where a supervised AI agent's cost and speed advantage is largest: inbox triage, CRM updates, scheduled research pulls, status reporting. That is also where an audit trail matters most for trusting the output. Client-facing judgment calls, sensitive conversations, and anything that depends on a relationship built over time are still better handled by a person.

Many teams end up running both: a human VA for relationship-dependent work, and an AI employee handling the recurring execution around it.
